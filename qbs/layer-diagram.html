Quiet Between Stars, pulled apart

One shot of deep space, built in After Effects from flat layers of gas and a field of stars where every star is its own shape layer, each set at its own distance in front of one camera. When the camera moves, every layer slides at a different speed. That difference is the depth.

    Flat cards, real depth

    Every layer is a flat 2D card placed in 3D space. Nothing is modelled. The depth comes only from each card's distance to the lens.

    The camera does the work

    The camera is the only thing that moves. Near layers cross the frame faster than far ones, and the eye reads that difference as depth.

    Every star is a layer

    Each star is its own shape layer with its own depth, not a dot painted onto one card. No two stars slide at quite the same speed, so the field reads as space instead of a backdrop.

    Far cards are bigger

    A card further back has to be larger to fill the same frame. That is why the back plate dwarfs the haze in the orbit view, and why everything lines up through the lens.

    Layer names are written for this diagram and follow what each layer does. Spacing and textures are drawn for it too. A diagram, not to scale.
