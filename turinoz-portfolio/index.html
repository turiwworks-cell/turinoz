Curve Lab

A compact curve editor and motion inspector I built for myself. Free to use, no login.

Role
Design and direction
Built with
Claude, over many rounds
Format
One HTML file
Status
Curve is stable, Inspect is in beta

Try it

Your curve cubic-bezier(0.20, 0.00, 0.00, 1.00)

Drag the two handles. The three objects replay with your curve.

Two workspaces

Curve

An everyday toolbox. The graph, sample objects and presets stay visible together, and it never fills the screen.

Inspect

Full screen and fixed. Open an HTML page, find what's animating, isolate one animation and retime it. In beta.

Who it's for

Motion designers who know easing but not code. It always shows what's selected and how to animate it.

Credits

Design and direction by Turinoz. Built with Claude over many rounds.

Spectra

A color-guessing toy. A color appears for a moment, you mix it back from memory with three ramps, then see how close you got.

Role
Idea, design, build
Tools
Rive scripting (Luau), web
Status
Telegram mini app in progress

How it plays

A color is shown briefly, then it's gone. You rebuild it with red, green and blue ramps. The score shows how close you came, with the color's details.

Where it came from

It began as a procedural color playground in Rive: palettes and harmony rules (analogous, complementary, triadic, monochromatic), all driven by scripting. It needed one clear idea, so it became a guessing game.

Look

Minimal and editorial, close to Swiss design. No faces and no characters. The color is the subject.

Next

A Telegram mini app, @colplaybot, rebuilt for the web without Rive, with a two-player challenge of three colors per run.

Live score concept

A match-day app concept built in Rive around one animated Live switch. Press it, and the list narrows to the matches being played right now.

Role
Concept, design, animation
Tools
Rive
Status
In progress

The switch

The Live icon came first, animated in Rive as vector. On its own it was too small to carry a piece, so the screens around it have to come alive too.

Screens

Match list, then a match, then its stats, each with its own motion. One full-screen artboard, taller than a phone screen but not a long scroll.

Rules

Readability first: show what actually needs attention. Then push past the habits of other score apps. The mood is cinematic, cosmic and quiet.

Process

The design is settled in an HTML prototype first, then built in Rive.
