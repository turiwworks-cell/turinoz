Scoreline, a football app for match nights, was an experiment: a way to test my own direction with AI as a new tool. I designed the layout, the colors and the motion.

01 — Tour

A quick tour.

The phone is the real app. It follows along as you scroll, and you can take over any time.

  1. 01Live

    It opens on what’s live.

    On a match night, that’s the first thing you want to know. Each card grows a little with every minute, so you can tell which games are nearly over.

  2. 02Following

    Follow one player.

    Every touch, live. Pick a player and they get their own card at the top.

  3. 03Match

    The match, as it happens.

    Facts first: the score and who’s on top. Then every event, minute by minute.

  4. 04Stats

    Stats, live.

    Every shot, corner, foul and more, counted as it happens.

  5. 05Player

    From the lineup to the player.

    See who’s playing where, then tap anyone for their own stats in this match.

  6. 06Goal

    Elsewhere, just a note.

    A goal or a red card in another match comes in quietly. Your match gets the whole screen.

02 — System

Black, five spectra, one typeface.

The look is small on purpose. Pure black for the night, five gradients for everything that’s alive, and one grotesk in five weights. Every value came from my prototype and lives in one tokens file.

04 — Rive

Two Rive files, built by me.

Rive draws only what it’s best at. The app talks to both files through view-model properties and nothing else.

Rive · live-icon.rivislive = true · count = "14"
The Live switch needs WebGL 2 to draw here. It runs inside the demo phone either way.
day 14
Rive 01 · The Live switch

A switch that gathers itself.

When Live turns on, the capsule draws in a little, opens past its mark and settles back. When it turns off, it swells first, then closes. It’s the only thing in the app allowed to overshoot: I animated it by hand, and wanted it to breathe rather than snap. The number inside is the calendar day.

Rive · moments.rivphase 0
The goal word needs WebGL 2 to draw here. Score a goal in the demo phone to see it in the app.
Rive 02 · Moments

GOAAAL, letter by letter.

Each letter comes in big and out of focus and lands sharp, one after another, while the word settles from a touch larger. When the last one lands, a soft flare blooms and a glint crosses it. The colors are the scoring team’s, made pastel. A red card slams in instead, in fixed reds.

05 — Process

I directed. The AI built.

Claude and Codex wrote 39,650 lines across 125 commits.

How one round went

25+AI sessions · 32 branches
  1. 01Me

    Decide

    The look, the timing, the rule. Written down so it can’t drift.

  2. 02AI

    Build

    The code, and a test that fails if my number changes.

  3. 03Me

    Review

    On a phone, frame by frame. Screenshots, videos, notes.

  4. 04Me → AI

    Ship or redo

    Back to build until I approve. Some things I reverted the next day.

Built with React 19, TypeScript and Vite. Motion for animation, Zustand for state, Zod for data, and the Rive runtime for the Live switch and the goal word.
